Watch the number that never comes down

Compare the two footers. in= went from 26 to 122, nearly five times, for a follow-up of six words. Your numbers will differ by a few tokens. The shape will not. Keep chatting and watch it climb. Send these one at a time, in this order:

What is the hardest part of building one?

And a counter-example.

Then look at exactly what is being re-sent:

/context

Every message, in order, with a token estimate against each one. Nothing is hidden and nothing is summarised. That whole panel is sent again with your next message.

💰 The commercial version of this slide. Cost and latency grow with the square of a conversation's length. Turn ten re-sends turns one through nine, and you pay for them again. When someone says their AI pilot “got expensive and slow once people really used it”, this is usually the mechanism.

4

Prove it is your code, not the model

In the chatbox, throw the transcript away:

/reset

Now ask the follow-up again. It has forgotten, completely. That is Lesson 01 behaviour, with no code change and no model change.

What looks like memory is a design decision made on your side of the API call. Which makes it a security decision too: what an agent keeps, how long it keeps it, and whose data ends up in it are all your choices, made in your code, and auditable by you.

5

Make the memory lie

Tell it something false, then ask about it two turns later. One at a time, in this order:

What is a context window?

It knows. Not because it remembers you, but because that sentence is still in the list being re-sent. Now /reset and ask the last question again. It does not know you at all.

Key takeaways

  • Memory is a list your code keeps. remember=True means “hold on to the transcript and send it again”. The model is exactly as stateless as it was in Lesson 01.
  • You pay for it on every turn. Turn ten re-sends turns one through nine, so cost and latency grow with the square of the conversation.
  • What an agent keeps is a design decision, so it is a security decision. What goes in, how long it stays, and whose data ends up in it are choices in your code. That makes them reviewable, and somebody should be reviewing them.
  • And everything in there is trusted. Nothing re-checks an entry and nothing records who put it there. That payload leaves your network on every turn, which is the traffic Prism AIRS inspects.
